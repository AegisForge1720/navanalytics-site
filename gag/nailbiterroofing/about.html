Our Origin Story

A completely made-up history, told with a completely straight face.

Nervous Neville Shingleton is a roofing nail, so he always assumed he would work with roofs. He loved the angles. He loved the tiles. He loved the way a good chimney sticks up proudly like a thumb. There was only one small problem, which he discovered on his very first day of drawing practice: roofs are up. They are always up. Nobody had warned him.

Neville stood at the foot of his first cartoon house for a very long time. He looked up. He gulped. He bit his fingernails, which is a complicated thing for a nail to do, and then his hard hat slipped down over his eyes. A passing cartoonist laughed, wrote "Nail Biter Roofing" on a sign, and the business was born.

He soon found others who shared his love of roofs and his preference for the ground. Wobbly Wanda, who has never wobbled once but likes to be prepared. Gutter Gus, who knows everything about gutters from looking at them through binoculars. Captain Cliff Hanger, the crew's narrator, who ends every sentence with a dramatic pause. And Mittens, the crew's cartoon cat, the only member who has ever been on a roof, and who refuses to talk about it.

Together they developed the Nail Biter Method: arrive, look up, worry beautifully, and end the episode before anything actually happens. Fans began tuning in just to find out how each pretend job would end. None of them ever did. The suspense became the product, and the roofs, sensibly, were left completely alone.

The early days were not easy. Neville's first business card was printed so small that he had to squint at it nervously. His first company van was a drawing of a van that someone had forgotten to give wheels. And his first motivational speech to the crew, written on the back of a shingle, simply read "Let us all look up, together, very slowly." It got a standing ovation, mostly because everyone was already standing. They looked up together. They gulped together. A team was born.

Today the crew remains proudly earthbound. They still gasp at every gable, still shiver at every shingle and still cut to black at the crucial moment. The company is entirely imaginary and always will be, but the domain is real, and it is waiting for a buyer with steady nerves. Or at least steadier than Neville's.
